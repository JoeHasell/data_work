Global Inequality Analysis

Why Do PIP and WID Tell Different Stories?

An investigation into the composition of global income inequality

There are two major sources of global data on incomes: WID and PIP. They tell very different stories about the nature of global income inequality.

Mean Log Deviation (MLD)

Measuring Overall Inequality

$$\text{MLD} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{p_i}{P} \ln\left(\frac{\bar{y}}{y_i}\right)$$
Where:
  • \(y_i\) = income of group \(i\)
  • \(\bar{y}\) = global mean income
  • \(p_i\) = population of group \(i\)
  • \(P\) = total global population

Each group's contribution is weighted by their share of the population. Higher values indicate more inequality.

MLD Decomposition

Between-Country and Within-Country Components

$$\text{MLD}_{\text{between}} = \sum_{c=1}^{C} \frac{P_c}{P} \ln\left(\frac{\bar{y}}{\bar{y}_c}\right)$$

Between-country: Inequality that would exist if everyone in each country earned their country's mean income (\(\bar{y}_c\))

$$\text{MLD}_{\text{within}} = \sum_{c=1}^{C} \frac{P_c}{P} \times \text{MLD}_c$$

Within-country: Average of each country's internal inequality (\(\text{MLD}_c\)), weighted by population share

$$\text{MLD}_{\text{total}} = \text{MLD}_{\text{between}} + \text{MLD}_{\text{within}}$$

Which Countries Drive Within-Country Inequality?

The within-country component (shown in colors) is a simple sum of contributions from individual countries.

China and India dominate in both datasets due to their large populations and moderate-to-high inequality.

Countries with smaller contributions are grouped as "Other countries" (gray) for clarity.

Comparing PIP with both WID per adult and WID per capita reveals the impact of the population basis adjustment.

WID's per capita adjustment increases the between-country share from 21.7% to 28.3%, but a substantial gap with PIP (69.2%) remains.

Adding WID post-tax income per capita reveals the impact of redistribution through the tax system.

Post-tax income shows a substantially higher between-country share (42.4%) compared to pre-tax (28.3%), though still well below PIP's 69.2%.

This suggests that within-country redistribution compresses inequality more than differences in mean income across countries.

Alternative 1: Replace zeros with $0.10/day

Alternative 2: Replace zeros with each country's lowest non-zero percentile value
